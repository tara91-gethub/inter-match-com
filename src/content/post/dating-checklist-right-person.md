Having a checklist when looking for a partner is normal. You want to understand someone's **values**, family, culture, career, lifestyle, future plans and many other things. Some of these questions are important, especially when you are seriously looking for marriage.

But at what point does a checklist stop helping you and start making it harder to actually know someone?

## Has Modern Dating Changed How We Choose People?

Modern dating gives people an enormous number of choices. Someone can look at 30 or 50 profiles in a short period of time and make a decision about another human being within two or three seconds.

Then comes another profile, another conversation and another option.

Add ghosting, benching and several conversations happening at the same time, and attention becomes divided. Sometimes people may barely remember someone they were speaking to a few weeks earlier.

We do many things to improve our focus. We go to the gym, practise yoga, learn dance or do other activities that require attention and consistency. Yet when it comes to dating, many people are training themselves to do the opposite, moving from one person to another extremely quickly.

That constant choice can make comparison automatic. Instead of asking, **"Do I want to understand this person better?"** people can start asking, **"Could the next person be better?"**

This is one reason we believe [private introductions can feel very different from dating apps](/blog/private-matchmaking-southeast-asia). Dating apps are often built around volume, while thoughtful matchmaking is built around judgment.

## A Profile Can Never Show the Whole Person

Maybe you do not like someone's photograph. Maybe you do not like the way they are standing. Their clothes may not be your style. Their cultural background may be different from what you originally imagined.

But none of these things can tell you what it feels like to sit across from that person.

A photograph cannot properly show **humour**, kindness, intelligence, confidence, warmth or how someone treats the people around them. Someone may have an average photograph but be completely different when you actually meet them.

Before turning a difference into a reason for concern, there is a useful question to ask.

Is this actually a problem, or is it simply something unfamiliar?

## People Can Introduce Each Other to a New World

Perhaps someone has never tried something that is important to you. That does not mean they will never enjoy it.

Maybe they have never travelled the way you travel. Maybe they have never eaten the food you love, experienced your culture, tried one of your hobbies or lived the kind of lifestyle you enjoy.

Why assume the answer will always be no?

Sometimes another person introduces us to parts of life we did not even know we would enjoy. You might introduce something completely new to them, and they might do the same for you.

People learn. People change. Their habits, views, priorities and lifestyles can change as their lives change.

## Compatibility Can Be Created

We often talk about **compatibility** as though it is something that must already exist when two people first meet.

But some compatibility is discovered, and some of it is created.

Two people learn each other's habits. They make adjustments. They develop routines. They discover what matters to the other person. Eventually, they begin creating their own way of living together.

In older styles of dating, people often had more attention available for one person. They had time to discover things gradually. A small difference did not always mean immediately looking for another option.

Sometimes the response could simply be, "You have never tried this? Come with me. Maybe you will like it."

That is how people discover each other.

## Remember That You Are Also on Their Checklist

There is another side to the checklist that we sometimes forget.

While you are deciding whether somebody satisfies all your requirements, that person is also deciding whether you satisfy theirs.

Maybe there are things about you that are not exactly what they imagined either.

That is normal.

A relationship cannot be one person arriving with a long list while expecting the other person to satisfy everything perfectly. Both people are bringing different strengths, weaknesses, habits and experiences into the relationship.

## Two Different People Will Never Be 100 Percent Compatible

You are two different human beings.

You have different brains, different families, different education, different experiences and different ways of thinking. Even your fingerprints are different.

So why would we expect two people to think, behave and live exactly the same way?

The aim should not be **100 percent compatibility**. That is probably an unrealistic expectation.

You do not have to agree on everything. You do not have to enjoy everything the other person enjoys. You do not have to become the same person.

You are two different people trying to build one life together beautifully.

That is the important part.

## Is It a Problem or Simply Something Unfamiliar?

Keep the things that genuinely matter to you. Serious incompatibilities should not be ignored.

But not every difference is a serious incompatibility.

Before rejecting someone because one part of the picture does not look exactly as you expected, ask yourself: is this actually a problem, or is it simply something unfamiliar?

Perhaps you have not experienced it yet. Perhaps they have not experienced your way of doing things yet. Perhaps both of you will change a little.

Finding the right person is not always about finding someone who arrives perfectly completed according to your checklist.

Sometimes compatibility is something two people discover.

And sometimes it is something they create together.
